Weakness is the single biggest damage modifier in the Pokémon TCG. It **doubles** the damage an attack does, which turns a solid hit into a one-hit Knock Out. Resistance is smaller but can still decide close games. This guide explains both, plus the types they depend on.

## The ten types

Current Pokémon cards use ten types, each shown by a symbol near the HP:

| Type | Typical Pokémon |
| --- | --- |
| Grass | Plants and bugs |
| Fire | Fire Pokémon |
| Water | Water and Ice Pokémon |
| Lightning | Electric Pokémon |
| Psychic | Psychic, Ghost and Fairy Pokémon |
| Fighting | Fighting, Ground and Rock Pokémon |
| Darkness | Dark and Poison Pokémon |
| Metal | Steel Pokémon |
| Dragon | Dragon Pokémon |
| Colorless | Normal and Flying Pokémon |

The card game has fewer types than the video games, so several video game types are folded together. Older cards also used a **Fairy** type, which was retired; Fairy Pokémon on current cards are usually Psychic.

**Basic Energy** exists for eight of the types. There is no Basic Dragon or Colorless Energy: Dragon Pokémon usually need two different Energy types, and Colorless costs can be paid with any Energy.

## Weakness: ×2

Weakness is printed at the bottom left of the card as a type symbol and **×2**. When an attacking Pokémon of that type does damage to this Pokémon while it is in the **Active Spot**, the damage is doubled.

Example: your Lightning Pokémon attacks for 110 against a Water Pokémon with Lightning Weakness. The attack does **220**, enough to Knock Out most Pokémon ex in one hit.

## Resistance: −30

Resistance is shown next to Weakness as a type symbol and **−30**. Damage from that type is reduced by 30. Many current cards have no Resistance, so it appears less often than Weakness.

Example: an attack for 60 against a Pokémon that resists its type does **30**.

## When they do not apply

- **Benched Pokémon**: damage to the Bench ignores Weakness and Resistance unless the attack says otherwise.
- **Damage counters**: effects that "put damage counters" rather than "do damage" skip Weakness and Resistance entirely.
- **Pokémon Checkup**: Poison and Burn damage are not attacks, so they ignore both.
- **The attacker's type, not the Energy**: Weakness depends on the **type of the attacking Pokémon**, not the type of Energy attached to it.

## The order matters

Weakness and Resistance are applied after effects on the attacking Pokémon and before effects on the defending Pokémon:

1. Base damage and the attack's text
2. Effects on the attacker (+)
3. **Weakness (×2)**
4. **Resistance (−30)**
5. Effects on the defender (−)

The worked examples are in [how damage is calculated](/en/guides/damage-calculation/), and the [damage calculator](/en/tools/damage-calculator/) does the maths for you.

## Common matchups

Weakness varies from card to card, so always read the card in front of you. That said, many recent cards follow these familiar patterns:

| Type | Often weak to |
| --- | --- |
| Grass | Fire |
| Fire | Water |
| Water | Lightning or Grass |
| Lightning | Fighting |
| Psychic | Darkness or Metal |
| Fighting | Grass or Psychic |
| Darkness | Grass or Fighting |
| Metal | Fire |
| Colorless | Fighting |
| Dragon | Usually no Weakness |

Treat this table as a starting point for spotting likely threats, not as a rule.

## Using Weakness in practice

### When building a deck

- Look at the most popular decks in your area. If many of them share a Weakness, a Pokémon of that type can be a strong **tech** card, even as a single copy.
- Check your own main attacker's Weakness. If a popular deck hits it for double, consider a backup attacker of a different type.
- Pokémon that can attack with Colorless Energy are easy to fit into almost any deck, which makes them common tech choices.

### During a game

- Before you attack, check the defending Pokémon's Weakness. Using a switching or "gust" effect to bring up a Benched Pokémon that is weak to you can turn a two-hit Knock Out into one.
- When you choose your Active Pokémon, avoid putting forward one that is weak to your opponent's main attacker if you have a choice.
- Remember Weakness when counting Prizes: a single doubled attack on a Pokémon ex can be worth 2 Prize cards.
