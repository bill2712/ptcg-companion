Every Pokémon TCG deck is a probability problem. You choose how many copies of each card to play, and the shuffle decides the rest. Knowing a few numbers makes it much easier to decide between a 3-of and a 4-of, or whether a single copy of a key card is too risky.

All numbers below are exact for a 60-card deck. To try your own deck, use the [probability calculator](/en/tools/probability-calculator/).

## Opening-hand odds

Your opening hand is 7 cards. The chance of having **at least one copy** of a card in it:

| Copies in deck | In opening 7 | By your first turn (8 cards) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 11.7% | 13.3% |
| 2 | 22.1% | 25.1% |
| 3 | 31.5% | 35.4% |
| 4 | 39.9% | 44.5% |

Even a 4-of is in your opening hand less than half the time. That is why consistent decks rely on **search cards** (cards that find a specific Pokémon or Trainer from the deck) and **draw Supporters**, rather than hoping to draw a single card naturally. Counting all the cards that can get you to a key piece gives a much better picture than counting the piece alone.

## How often cards are prized

You set aside 6 Prize cards from your deck, so on average 10% of your deck is in the Prizes. For a single card, the chance that at least one copy is prized:

| Copies in deck | At least 1 prized | All copies prized |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 10.0% | 10.0% |
| 2 | 19.2% | 0.85% |
| 3 | 27.5% | 0.058% |
| 4 | 35.1% | 0.003% |

A single copy of a key card is stuck in your Prizes in **1 game out of 10**. With 2 copies, losing both is under 1%. This is the main reason most players play at least 2 copies of anything their deck cannot function without, and a common reason to include cards that let you look at or take Prize cards.

## Mulligan rates

If your opening 7 cards have no Basic Pokémon, you must mulligan: reveal, reshuffle and draw 7 again, and your opponent may draw an extra card. Your mulligan rate depends on how many Basic Pokémon your deck runs:

| Basic Pokémon | Chance of a mulligan |
| --- | --- |
| 8 | 34.6% |
| 10 | 25.9% |
| 12 | 19.1% |
| 14 | 13.9% |
| 16 | 9.9% |
| 20 | 4.8% |

Some cards let you search for a Basic Pokémon but do not count as one themselves, so they do not reduce your mulligan rate. Only the Basic Pokémon cards count.

## Putting it together

- **Count outs, not copies.** If three different cards can all find your main attacker, you effectively have more than four copies.
- **Two copies is the minimum for a must-have card**, because a single copy is prized 10% of the time.
- **A 3-of versus a 4-of** changes your opening odds by about 8 percentage points. That is often worth a slot for your most important early card and rarely worth it for a late-game card.
- **Very low Basic counts** save space but cost games. If you run fewer than 10 Basics, expect to mulligan in about a quarter of your games.
