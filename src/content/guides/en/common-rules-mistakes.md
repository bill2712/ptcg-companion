Most rules disputes at a kitchen table or a local league come from the same short list of mistakes. Some give one player an unfair advantage; others just slow the game down. Here are the fifteen we see most, grouped by when they happen, with the correct rule for each.

## Setting up

### 1. Drawing Prize cards before checking for Basic Pokémon

The correct order is: draw 7, place your Basic Pokémon, mulligan if you have none, and **only then** set aside 6 Prize cards. If you set Prizes first and then mulligan, you have to undo everything. See [going first and mulligans](/en/guides/going-first-and-mulligans/).

### 2. Forgetting the opponent's extra cards after a mulligan

When your opponent mulligans, **you** may draw one extra card for each of their mulligans. It is optional, but most players take it. If both players mulligan, the shared mulligans cancel out.

## The first turn

### 3. Attacking when going first

The player who goes first **cannot attack** on their first turn. This applies even if they have the Energy for an attack that costs nothing.

### 4. Playing a Supporter when going first

The first player also **cannot play a Supporter** on their first turn. Items, Stadiums, Tools, Abilities, Energy attachment and retreating are all still allowed.

### 5. Evolving on the first turn

**Neither player** can evolve on their own first turn, and nobody can evolve a Pokémon the turn it was put into play. The player going second also has to wait until their second turn to evolve.

## Every turn

### 6. Forgetting to draw at the start of the turn

Every turn begins with a draw, including the first player's first turn. If you realise you forgot, tell your opponent straight away; it is much easier to fix early.

### 7. Attaching more than one Energy

You can attach **one Energy card from your hand per turn**, to any one of your Pokémon. Abilities and attacks that attach Energy are separate and do not count towards this.

### 8. Playing a second Supporter

Only **one Supporter per turn**. If you play a Supporter and it draws you into a better one, the better one has to wait until next turn.

### 9. Retreating twice

You can **retreat only once per turn**. Switching with a card effect, such as an Item that switches your Active Pokémon, does not count as retreating, so you can still do both. See [retreating and switching](/en/guides/retreat-and-switching/).

### 10. Playing a Stadium with the same name

You cannot play a Stadium if one **with the same name** is already in play, even if it is your opponent's. You can play a different Stadium to replace it.

## Damage

### 11. Applying Weakness and Resistance to Benched Pokémon

Weakness and Resistance only apply to damage to the **Active** Pokémon. When an attack also does damage to the Bench, that damage ignores Weakness and Resistance unless the attack says otherwise. More in [how damage is calculated](/en/guides/damage-calculation/).

### 12. Applying effects in the wrong order

Work out damage in this order: base damage and the attack's own text, then effects on the attacker, then **Weakness (×2)**, then **Resistance (−30)**, then effects on the defending Pokémon. Doing Weakness last can change the result by a lot. The [damage calculator](/en/tools/damage-calculator/) applies the order for you.

## Special Conditions

### 13. Keeping Special Conditions after evolving or retreating

When a Pokémon **evolves**, or **moves to the Bench** (by retreating or switching), all of its Special Conditions are removed. An Asleep or Paralyzed Pokémon cannot retreat, but a switching card can still move it. See [Special Conditions explained](/en/guides/special-conditions/).

### 14. Skipping Pokémon Checkup

Poison and Burn damage, and the coin flips to wake up or recover from a Burn, happen in **Pokémon Checkup after every turn**, not just your own. Paralysis ends during the Checkup after its owner's next turn. Leaving these until "later" is how games end with the wrong HP.

## Prize cards

### 15. Taking the wrong number of Prizes

A Pokémon ex gives up **2 Prizes**, a Mega Evolution Pokémon ex gives up **3**, and most others give up **1**. The rule box on the card tells you. When several Pokémon are Knocked Out at once, the attacking player takes Prizes for each. See [Prize cards and winning](/en/guides/prize-cards-and-winning/).

## How to avoid them

- **Say your actions out loud**: "attach Energy, play Supporter, attack." Your opponent can catch a mistake before it matters.
- **Use a tracker.** [PTCG Companion](/en/) has toggles for Energy, Supporter, Retreat and Ability that reset when you tap **Turn ➔**, runs Pokémon Checkup for you, and shows each player's Prizes.
- **Fix mistakes immediately.** In casual play, players usually rewind a small mistake if it is spotted straight away. At an event, call a judge rather than fixing it yourselves.
