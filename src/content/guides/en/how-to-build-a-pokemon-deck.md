Building your own deck is where the Pokémon TCG really opens up. It can also feel overwhelming: there are thousands of cards and only 60 slots. The trick is to build around **one clear plan** and fill the rest with cards that help that plan happen every game. This guide gives you a step-by-step method and a template you can adapt.

## The deck rules

A deck for normal play must follow these rules:

- **Exactly 60 cards.**
- **No more than 4 copies** of any card with the same name, except **Basic Energy**, which is unlimited.
- **At least 1 Basic Pokémon.** In practice you want many more.
- **Only 1 ACE SPEC card** in the whole deck.
- For tournaments, every card must be legal in the format. See [Standard, Expanded and rotation](/en/guides/standard-expanded-and-rotation/).

## Step 1: Pick one main attacker

Choose the Pokémon that will do most of your attacking. Good first choices:

- Have an attack that does a lot of damage for **two or three Energy**.
- Are a **Basic or Stage 1** Pokémon, which are easier to set up than Stage 2.
- Have high HP so they survive a hit.

Then add **one partner** that supports it, for example a Pokémon with an Ability that draws cards or accelerates Energy. A deck that does one thing well wins more than a deck that does five things badly.

## Step 2: Build the evolution lines

If your attacker evolves, you need enough of each stage. Common ratios:

| Line | Common counts | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic → Stage 1 | 4–3 or 3–3 | You need the Basic early and often. |
| Basic → Stage 1 → Stage 2 | 4–2–3 or 4–3–3 | Fewer Stage 1 if you play Items that skip it. |
| Single Basic attacker | 3–4 copies | Enough to open with and to replace one after a Knock Out. |

Thin lines (1–1) look efficient but often fail because one card is in your Prizes or is discarded early.

## Step 3: Add Trainers for consistency

Trainers are what make your deck work every game rather than one game in three. Most decks play **30–38 Trainer cards**, spread across:

- **Draw Supporters (8–12)**: Supporters that draw several cards or replace your hand. You want to play one almost every turn.
- **Pokémon search (6–10)**: Items that find Basic or evolution Pokémon from your deck.
- **Energy search or acceleration (2–6)**: cards that find Energy or attach extra Energy.
- **Switching (2–4)**: Items that switch your Active Pokémon. See [retreating and switching](/en/guides/retreat-and-switching/).
- **Gust effects (1–3)**: cards that bring an opposing Benched Pokémon into the Active Spot, so you can Knock Out the right target.
- **Stadium and Tools (2–5)**: optional, for extra damage, HP or disruption.
- **Your ACE SPEC (0–1)**.

## Step 4: Add Energy

Most decks play **8–14 Energy cards**. Use the lower end if your deck has strong Energy search or acceleration, and the higher end if you rely on attaching one Energy per turn from your hand. Count the Energy your main attack needs: a three-Energy attack needs more Energy in the deck than a one-Energy attack.

If your deck uses two types, start with roughly the same ratio as the costs of your attacks.

## A starting template

Use this as a frame and adjust it to your cards:

| Section | Cards |
| --- | --- |
| Main attacker line | 7–10 |
| Support Pokémon (draw, Energy, pivot) | 3–6 |
| Draw Supporters | 8–12 |
| Pokémon search Items | 6–10 |
| Switching and gust | 4–6 |
| Other Trainers (Stadium, Tools, ACE SPEC) | 3–6 |
| Energy | 8–14 |
| **Total** | **60** |

A typical finished list lands around **14–18 Pokémon, 32–36 Trainers and 9–12 Energy**.

## Step 5: Check your Basic Pokémon count

Count every **Basic Pokémon** in the deck. Fewer than about 10 means you will often mulligan; more than about 16 can leave too little room for Trainers. Our [deck-building probability guide](/en/guides/deck-building-probability/) shows exact mulligan rates, and the [probability calculator](/en/tools/probability-calculator/) checks your numbers.

## Step 6: Test, then cut and add

Play at least five to ten games before making big changes. After each game, note:

- Did you **open with a Basic** and set up by turn two or three?
- Which cards **sat in your hand** all game? Those are cut candidates.
- Which card did you **wish you had**? That is what to add.
- Did you **run out of Energy** or have too much?

Change only two to four cards at a time so you can tell what made the difference.

## Common first-deck mistakes

- **Too many different Pokémon**, one or two copies each. Pick one plan.
- **Too few draw Supporters.** You need one almost every turn.
- **Too much Energy**, which leaves no room for search.
- **Only Stage 2 attackers** with no way to find them.
- **No switching cards**, so one Asleep Pokémon ruins a game.
